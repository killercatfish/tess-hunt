## Three things to know

1. **Survivors that turn up late, behind deep or many signals.** On known binary TIC 1220444 (7.16 d, 4.7%
   deep) the 5th pass now finds a 2.58 d signal that survives every test; it is not at the binary's period,
   so the binary count above does not see it. The new pilot survivor, TIC 278593441 at 36.83 d, is the 8th
   signal on a star whose first 7 (21 to 39 d) were all rejected, with SNR 7.0. Both look like leftovers
   from masking, not planets. This is a new way for a false signal through, made by searching longer.
2. **The lost pilot survivor is not the n5 change.** TIC 243514635 at 27.95 d (14 Earth radii) has the
   same numbers in both runs. It now fails sector consistency: depths of 106,000, 64,000 and 37,000 ppm in
   sectors 55, 81 and 82. The old pilot result predates 12560cc (10/10), which drops the worst sector only
   when there are 4 or more; with 3 sectors the old run dropped sector 55 and passed it. The rejection is
   right under the current rules. It also means the old pilot baseline was made with older kill tests.
3. **The search costs about 2.2 times as much.** Pilot CPU went from 13.9 to 30.9 hours. The e7 estimate
   for re-searching eumemic's ~11,200 early-stop stars (5.5 to 11 days on 6 workers, at 254 s per star)
   becomes roughly 12 to 24 days.

## Follow-up (10/26, Josh approved)

Point 1 is now a soft flag, `late`: a survivor found after 3 or more rejected signals on its star is ranked
lower, never rejected (CHANGELOG 2026-10-26). Replayed on these results it flags exactly TIC 1220444 at 2.58 d,
TIC 278593441 at 36.83 d and eumemic's TIC 247166992 at 10.17 d; no known planet is flagged.
